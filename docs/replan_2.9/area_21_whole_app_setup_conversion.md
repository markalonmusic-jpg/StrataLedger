## 3. Stale-save refusal and `row_version` (owner rule g4 with A21-Q7 and Q8; the helper and wording are Planning readings, owner may correct)

- **Column (owner A21-Q7: every editable table, including tables only the system edits):** `row_version INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1` on **every editable table**: each area lists its own tables (Areas 9, 11, 14, 16 and 20 name them); this area adds it to `company_settings` and any table an area did not list. **Insert-only tables, ledger tables and append-only tables are exempt** (`journal_line`, `audit_log`, history tables, `depreciation_run`, `custom_field_legacy`).
- **Helper (`tx.py`, so every service shares it; H4: no trigger):** `tx.update_versioned(conn, table, key_column, key, expected_version, changes)` runs `UPDATE ... SET ..., row_version = row_version + 1 WHERE key = ? AND row_version = ?`; zero rows changed re-reads the row: missing -> the typed not-found error; present -> **`StaleVersionError`** (new, in `errors.py`, carries the table, key, the version sent and the current version, no values). The API bridge maps it to `conflict` (contracts, `docs/contracts/reports.md:1315`); the page shows "Changed by someone else. Reload."
- **Calling rule:** every service update takes `expected_version`; a missing value is a programming error (static test). The authorization check (`authorize`, rule 10) runs before the version check, so a user without permission never learns the version.
- **Multi-row saves:** a Save that applies several edits in one transaction (Area 16's Custom fields Save, Area 18's role page) checks every row's version first and refuses the **whole** Save when any is stale and the page reloads (owner A21-Q8: all or nothing).
- **Audit:** the stale refusal writes nothing to the audit log (no change was made); the log keeps the successful updates with `row_version` in the changed-field set.
- **Worked example (integer cents):** invoice draft line price 450000 ($4,500.00), version 3. Dana loads it and saves 475000 ($4,750.00): version 4, a change of +25000. Marcus loaded version 3 and saves 460000 ($4,600.00): refused with `StaleVersionError` (sent 3, current 4); the stored price stays 475000; after Reload he sees 475000 and may save 460000 as version 5 (a change of -15000). No ledger line is written by either refused or accepted save; money stays integer cents.
- **Second example (owner A21-Q7, integer cents not involved):** Dana saves payment term 'Net 45' (version 1 -> 2); Sam's older screen saves 'Net 60' with version 1: refused with `StaleVersionError`, and the screen reloads showing Net 45. The all-or-nothing Save (A21-Q8): three Custom fields edits, the second changed by Dana a minute ago -> none save.
- **Not covered:** a read never bumps a version; a `company_settings` row version changes on any setting change; the AI agent's writes use the same helper (and the agent has no write permission).

## 5. Opening balances and conversion (owner rules: items 5 and 6, E4, R55, A21-Q3 to Q5b, Q9; the structure around them is a Planning reading, owner may correct)

- **Setting:** `company_settings.first_live_date` (DATE, NULL until set). **The conversion date is `first_live_date - 1 day`** (owner item 5): Jan 1, 2027 -> Dec 31, 2026; Mar 15, 2027 -> Mar 14, 2027. The new-company wizard's "Start date" is this field; it can be changed only before the conversion batch is posted.
- **Two conversion methods (owner A21-Q5):** (a) **import all existing transactions** (each imported transaction is its own linked entry, item 6 holds); (b) **import trial balances as journal entries**. The conversion amounts follow the method the company uses. **With (b) the company chooses (owner A21-Q5b):** one trial balance at the conversion date, or one per past month from the fiscal year start. *Planning reading (owner may correct):* monthly trial balances are cumulative, so each month's entry posts only that month's change (this month's balance minus last month's); open invoices, bills, assets and loans still come over as their own linked entries (item 6), and the trial-balance entry holds only what those do not cover; a 'Trial balance' `item_kind` with its `period_end` date carries each month.
- **`import_batch.batch_type` gains 'Opening balances' (Area 19 shares the batch columns).** One batch per company, status Draft -> In review -> Approved -> Posted. **R55 (owner):** typed and imported balances are both held for one human review of the exact items (digest as in Area 19 §5); saving creates an Import-origin draft and posts nothing; a separate reviewer is required only when the Controls setting "separation of duties" is ON (OFF by default).
- **`conversion_item` (new, staging):** `item_id`, `batch_id`, `item_kind` ('Account balance', 'Open invoice', 'Open bill', 'Fixed asset', 'Asset opening depreciation', 'Loan', 'Job started', 'Bank item'), `source_ref` (the invoice or bill number, asset code, account number), `account_id`, `contact_id`, `document_date`, `due_date`, `amount_cents` (integer; the sign is the account's normal side), `clearing_id` (a clearing identity per open document, owner OPENBAL-02), `entry_id` (set at posting), `row_version`. Open invoices and bills are real documents with their original numbers, contacts, due dates and open cents, flagged `is_opening` (the mockup text: "can be paid, credited or written off like any invoice").
- **Posting (finalization):** one write transaction, retry-safe by `operation_id` (OPENBAL-04): refuse unless the trial balance debits equal credits and each subledger ties to its control account (AR open items = account 1200; AP = 2000; assets and loans tie to Area 11's schedules); then post **one 'Conversion' entry per item**, dated the conversion date, each against the Opening Balance Equity account (`opening_balance_equity_acct_id`); then post the OBE close (below). The batch shows the actual posted entries, and the page opens the real entry (OPENBAL-03).
- **Opening Balance Equity (owner A21-Q4):** the equity accounts of the old books (owner's capital or common stock, retained earnings) come over as **their own item entries** (`item_kind` 'Equity balance'), which is the normal way for OBE to reach 0. **If OBE is not 0 when the batch is finalized, the app shows a hard warning with an explanation and a choice: bring OBE to 0 now, or the accountant clears it after the conversion is complete.** *Planning reading (owner may correct):* the conversion is not blocked; leftover OBE is **never moved to Retained earnings automatically**; choosing "accountant clears it later" is recorded on the batch with who chose it and when (`import_batch.obe_choice`, `obe_choice_by`, `obe_choice_at`), and it stays an open item in the month-end review (Area 13) until OBE is 0. This replaces the draft's automatic OBE-to-Retained-earnings entry (the earlier proposal is withdrawn).
- **Income and expense at conversion (owner A21-Q5):** nothing rolls into Retained earnings unless the conversion date is the end of the company's fiscal or calendar year. Jun 30 with Jan to Jun revenue 30,000,000 and expenses 26,000,000: the revenue and expense balances come over as they are (profit 30,000,000 - 26,000,000 = 4,000,000 stays in the P&L accounts, not in Retained earnings). Dec 31 (year end): the balance sheet comes over and the year's profit is already in Retained earnings.
- **Period handling:** the period containing the conversion date closes after posting for a month-end go-live (the mockup text "Period 2026-09 is closed after posting"); **a mid-month go-live is allowed (owner A21-Q9):** the conversion posts the day before go-live and Area 13's **Special cutoff** (a custom close date with a reason) closes through that day; the period stays open for the days from the first live date and the full close still happens at month end (03/15/2027 go-live: conversion 03/14/2027, March closed through the 14th).
- **Assets and loans (Area 11):** an opening asset creates its `fixed_asset` row with the cost and the opening accumulated depreciation; the schedule continues from the first live date. Because item 6 forbids a summary entry, the opening accumulated depreciation posts **as one entry per asset, linked to the asset** (owner A21-Q3; this replaces Area 11's "one opening entry" proposal, §12 item 1). An opening loan creates its `loan` with the principal at the conversion date and its schedule lines.
- **Worked example (all integer cents; trial-balance method with the open items as their own entries; first live date 01/01/2027, conversion date 12/31/2026, a year end):**

  | Item | Entry on 12/31/2026 | Amount |
  |------|---------------------|--------|
  | Operating checking | Dr Bank / Cr Opening Balance Equity | 5,000,000 |
  | Open invoice INV-0381 | Dr Accounts receivable / Cr OBE | 1,200,000 |
  | Open invoice INV-0380 | Dr Accounts receivable / Cr OBE | 800,000 |
  | Truck, cost | Dr Fixed assets / Cr OBE | 5,840,000 |
  | Truck, opening accumulated depreciation | Dr OBE / Cr Accumulated depreciation | 3,114,667 |
  | Open bill B-1 | Dr OBE / Cr Accounts payable | 900,000 |
  | Open bill B-2 | Dr OBE / Cr Accounts payable | 400,000 |
  | Equipment loan | Dr OBE / Cr Loans payable | 3,000,000 |
  | Owner's capital (old books) | Dr OBE / Cr Owner's capital | 1,000,000 |
  | Retained earnings (old books) | Dr OBE / Cr Retained earnings | 4,425,333 |

  Each entry balances (debits = credits) on its own. Ten item entries (eight asset, liability and bank items, then the two equity items). OBE credits 5,000,000 + 1,200,000 + 800,000 + 5,840,000 = 12,840,000; OBE debits before the equity items 3,114,667 + 900,000 + 400,000 + 3,000,000 = 7,414,667; **OBE balance 5,425,333 credit** = assets (5,000,000 + 1,200,000 + 800,000 + 5,840,000 - 3,114,667 = 9,725,333) - liabilities (900,000 + 400,000 + 3,000,000 = 4,300,000). The equity items debit OBE 1,000,000 + 4,425,333 = 5,425,333, so **OBE ends at 0** (credits 12,840,000 = debits 7,414,667 + 5,425,333 = 12,840,000) and nothing moves to Retained earnings automatically. If the Owner's capital and Retained earnings entries were not in the batch, OBE would stay 5,425,333 and the hard warning of A21-Q4 would show with its choice. The truck's book value is 5,840,000 - 3,114,667 = 2,725,333 ($27,253.33), as in Area 11.

## 7. Entities inside one company file and consolidation (owner: design it here, A21-Q1, Q2, Q2b; the structure around them is a Planning reading, owner may correct)

- **Today:** `company_entity` (code, name, `parent_entity_id`, `is_active`, `is_eliminations`) and `journal_line.entity_id` (NULL = the file's own company) exist. Nothing else uses them; Area 17 refuses `entity_mode`; the screens stay hidden until a second entity exists.
- **Proposals:** (1) **entity balance (owner A21-Q1):** inside one journal entry the debits equal the credits **per entity** (`entity_id` NULL counts as the file's own company), so moving money between entities needs both sides; (2) **intercompany accounts:** two system roles, `intercompany_receivable` (due from) and `intercompany_payable` (due to), with the counterparty entity on the line (`journal_line.counterparty_entity_id`, NULL otherwise); (3) **Eliminations (owner A21-Q2):** the `is_eliminations` entity holds elimination entries only; the month-end review (Area 13) shows an **auto-generated draft** of the eliminations built from the intercompany pairs (`consolidation.draft_eliminations(context, period)`, planning reading), and the entries **post only after review and approval**; a posted elimination is corrected only by a reversal (rule 8); (4) **documents:** a job (`project.entity_id`) and its documents and costs belong to one entity; a document without a job belongs to the file's own company unless an entity is chosen; (5) **reports:** a consolidated view sums every entity plus the Eliminations entity, with an entity filter; `entity_mode` is lifted only after approval.
- **Intercompany charge through AR and AP (owner A21-Q2, Q2b, Q2c):** Due to and Due from come through AP and AR, and the charge is created **when the bill is paid or entered**, not at month end. **Two cases, both supported (owner Q2c, like SAP's paying company code and cross-company code posting):** **Case 1, payment for a related entity:** the supplier bill stays in the books of the entity it belongs to (WEC); another entity (WSB) pays it. **Case 2, bill coded to a related entity:** the bill is entered in the books of the entity that owes the supplier (WSB) with the expense coded to the other entity (WEC). *Planning reading (owner may correct):* (1) the **Due to and Due from lines come from clearing accounts set per entity pair** (a small settings table, like SAP's OBYA account setup: for the pair WSB-WEC, the account holding WSB's Due from WEC and the account holding WEC's Due to WSB; falling back to the `intercompany_receivable` and `intercompany_payable` roles); (2) the AR invoice from the payer to the other entity (owner Q2b) is the payer's Due from item (its posting is the entry's own Due from debit, not a second posting) and the AP bill in the other entity is the Due to item; (3) a service `intercompany.cross_charge` runs in the payment's or the bill's own transaction, retry-safe by `operation_id`; (4) case 1 must never count the expense twice: WEC's debit is its supplier AP, not Supplies; whether the charge itself needs approval is not decided (§13).
- **Permissions:** `entity.manage` (sensitive, Admin and Master); entity screens need at least two entities; deactivating an entity with posted lines keeps its history.
- **Worked example (integer cents):** parent WSB lends its subsidiary WEC 1,000,000 ($10,000.00). One entry, four lines: WSB Dr Due from WEC 1,000,000 / Cr Bank 1,000,000 (entity WSB: debits = credits); WEC Dr Bank 1,000,000 / Cr Due to WSB 1,000,000 (entity WEC: debits = credits). The elimination (an auto-generated draft in the month-end review, posted on approval) on the Eliminations entity ELIM: Dr Due to WSB 1,000,000 / Cr Due from WEC 1,000,000 (counterparties WSB and WEC). **Consolidated:** Due from WEC 1,000,000 - 1,000,000 = 0; Due to WSB 1,000,000 - 1,000,000 = 0; Bank 1,000,000 - 1,000,000 = 0 net of the two entities; every entity and the entry balance.
- **Worked example, case 1: payment for a related entity (owner A21-Q2, Q2b, Q2c; integer cents). Whose books hold the bill: WEC's.** WEC entered supplier bill SB-1 for supplies of 50,000 ($500.00): **WEC** Dr Supplies expense 50,000 / Cr AP-supplier 50,000. In October **WSB** pays it from its bank: **WSB** Dr Due from WEC 50,000 / Cr Bank 50,000; **WEC** Dr AP-supplier 50,000 / Cr Due to WSB 50,000 (this clears WEC's supplier bill; the expense is not counted twice). Each entity balances in every entry (50,000 = 50,000). After the payment: WEC Supplies 50,000, AP-supplier 0, Due to WSB 50,000; WSB Bank -50,000, Due from WEC 50,000. **Month-end elimination draft, approved in the review:** ELIM Dr Due to WSB 50,000 / Cr Due from WEC 50,000, which nets the dues to 0. **Consolidated:** Supplies 50,000, Bank -50,000, AP-supplier 0, Due from 50,000 - 50,000 = 0, Due to 50,000 - 50,000 = 0: one 50,000 expense paid from one bank account. When WEC repays WSB, WEC pays down its Due to WSB and WSB receives against its Due from WEC.
- **Worked example, case 2: bill coded to a related entity (owner Q2c; integer cents). Whose books hold the bill: WSB's.** WSB enters supplier bill SB-2 of 50,000 ($500.00) and codes the expense to WEC: **WSB** Dr Due from WEC 50,000 / Cr AP-supplier 50,000; **WEC** Dr Supplies expense 50,000 / Cr Due to WSB 50,000. Each entity balances. WSB later pays the supplier: **WSB** Dr AP-supplier 50,000 / Cr Bank 50,000 (no Due line is posted at payment, because the charge was made at bill entry). After the payment: WSB AP-supplier 0, Bank -50,000, Due from WEC 50,000; WEC Supplies 50,000, Due to WSB 50,000. The elimination draft is the same ELIM Dr Due to WSB 50,000 / Cr Due from WEC 50,000. **Consolidated:** Supplies 50,000, Bank -50,000, AP-supplier 0, dues 0: the same result as case 1.
